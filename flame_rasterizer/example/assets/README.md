# The example's SVGs

The SVGs in `svgs/` come from [flame_extended_svg](https://github.com/adario/flame_extended_svg), the Flame SVG
showcase that this example follows. Their author is this repository's, and they're under this repository's MIT
licence, except Svg Shooter's `rocket_ship.svg`, `missile.svg` and `spaceship_game.svg`, and `tiger.svg`.

The SVGs in `svgs/wireframe/` are Svg Shooter's, made from the SVGs of the same names in `svgs/`. Each is its original's
silhouette and a few of its shapes, stroked in white, which the game tints. The missile is filled in pale orange,
`#F5B58A`. Each wireframe is under its original's terms.

## rocket_ship.svg, missile.svg and spaceship_game.svg: CC0

`rocket_ship.svg`, `missile.svg` and `spaceship_game.svg` come from SVG Repo (https://www.svgrepo.com), under CC0 1.0, a
public domain dedication: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/. They need no attribution. Svg Shooter's
`spaceship_game.svg` is stroked in orange, `#F09050`, in a viewBox cropped to its ship.

## tiger.svg: separately licensed

`tiger.svg` is the Ghostscript tiger, the same file as `rasterizer_gpu/example/assets/tiger/tiger.svg`: an SVG
conversion of Ghostscript's `examples/tiger.eps`. Its licence isn't this repository's. Wikimedia Commons lists the
artwork as **AGPL-3.0-or-later**, as a Ghostscript example:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ghostscript_Tiger.svg. The AGPL's text is at
https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html. An app that bundles it, as this example does, must comply with that licence.
